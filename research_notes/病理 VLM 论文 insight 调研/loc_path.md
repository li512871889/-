# LoC-Path (Learning to Compress for Pathology Multimodal LLMs) — insight-focused notes

Scope note: all "v4" citations refer to the arXiv HTML full text of the latest version (v4, 19 Nov 2026), which I read in full including the supplementary (Sec. A–H). v4 differs materially from the Dec-2025 v1/v2 (new baselines, bootstrap CIs, CAMELYON16 stress test, toned-down claims), so numbers quoted from older summaries/aggregators may not match.

## 1. Exactly which paper is "LoC-Path"? (identity, authors, venue, code)

### Takeaway
"loc-path" is unambiguously **"LoC-Path: Learning to Compress for Pathology Multimodal Large Language Models"** (arXiv:2512.05391, Stony Brook / Chao Chen's group, accepted to WACV 2027). "LoC" = **L**earning t**o** **C**ompress, not "localization"; I found no pathology VLM paper named "Loc-Path"/"LocPath" about localization.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Confidence that the researcher meant arXiv:2512.05391 is very high: exact name match, pathology MLLM domain, timing, and the official repo is literally named `loc_path`.
- The paper sits in a lineage from the same lab: TCP-LLaVA (compress tokens with a Q-Former before a LLaVA-style LLM) → LoC-Path (compress *and* change the fusion interface to routed cross-attention).

### Gaps
- No OpenReview page or public reviews found (WACV does not publish reviews); reviewer critiques are therefore unavailable. The v2→v4 changes (see Sec. 4/5) are the best available proxy for what reviewers pushed on, but that is my inference, not documented.
- WACV 2027 acceptance is stated only in the arXiv comment field; no proceedings page exists yet.

## 2. What is the motivating observation?

### Takeaway
The authors argue slide-level MLLMs live in a regime unlike natural-image VLMs — extremely long visual sequence, weak supervision (thousands of tiles ↔ one report), sparse diagnostic evidence — and show empirically (Fig. 1, ~2k TCGA slides, CONCH-v1 features) that tile tokens are redundant globally and locally while only a minority align with the report text. Hence "the standard assumption that most visual tokens are worth preserving and jointly attending with text" breaks.

### Cited Findings
- Regime statement (verbatim): "whole-slide MLLMs operate in a regime that differs fundamentally from standard vision-language settings: the visual input sequence is extremely long (often thousands of tile tokens), slide-level text supervision is weak (many tiles map to one report), and diagnostically relevant evidence is sparse." — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Abstract framing: WSI MLLMs are hard "because gigapixel slides induce thousands of highly redundant visual tokens that must be learned from sparse slide-level language supervision"; "WSI tile features are highly redundant at both global and local scales, while task-relevant evidence is sparse and query-dependent." — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05391)
- Issue 1 (redundancy), verbatim: "a small set of K ≈ 250 prototype groups can fairly well reconstruct the entire tile feature set; and … tile features are highly similar to each other, as observed by comparing neighboring tiles and comparing pairs of tiles far apart." — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- How Fig. 1 is computed (Supp. B): (a) per-slide K-means with K ∈ {8,…,256}, normalized reconstruction MSE vs. K; (b) fraction of tiles whose nearest spatial neighbour has cosine > 0.90/0.95/0.99; (c) histogram of cosine over up to 50k random tile pairs per slide; (d) cosine κ_i between each tile feature and the slide's report embedding (cleaned WSI-LLaVA reports), tiles sorted by κ, cumulative share of *positive* similarity mass. — [arXiv HTML v4, Supp. B](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Issue 2 (task irrelevance) key numbers: 7.7% / 22.8% / 35.2% of tokens account for 50% / 80% / 90% of the positive report-similarity mass; "a majority of visual tokens are irrelevant to the pathology report. This aligns with how pathologists rely on key areas of WSIs for diagnosis." — [arXiv HTML v4, Fig. 1(d) + Sec. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Why existing designs are "wrong" per the authors: (i) LLaVA-style late fusion (SlideChat, WSI-LLaVA) concatenates a long visual prefix and runs self-attention over text+vision, O((T+L)^2) per layer; (ii) Q-Former-bottleneck variants (ALPaCA, TCP-LLaVA) cut tokens but "still operate under the same WSI regime of extreme sequence length, weak slide-text alignment, and sparse task-relevant evidence"; (iii) on the vision side, a slide-level encoder is "an additional heavy model stacked on the tile encoder", costly in memory/FLOPs, making end-to-end development hard under limited compute. — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Earlier (v2) wording of the same motivation, more rhetorical: "are these thousands of tokens really necessary? The answer, we find, is 'no'"; "Unlike human experts who primarily rely on key areas to arrive at the diagnosis, existing slide-level MLLMs … process thousands of tile/patch features in a brute-force manner." — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v2)

### Inferences
- The motivating evidence is entirely **feature-space statistics on frozen CONCH-v1 embeddings**, not an analysis of how the LLM uses visual tokens. Unlike FastV-style work, there is no LLM attention-map / layer-wise visual-attention analysis; the request's "how the LLM actually uses visual tokens" question is not addressed by this paper.
- "35.2% of tokens for 90% of mass" is a fairly mild sparsity statement (a perfectly uniform relevance profile would need 90%); the stronger number is 7.7% → 50%. And the proxy (cosine of a tile embedding to one global report embedding, positive part only) measures report-alignment of CONCH features, not causal task relevance.
- Fig. 1(d) supports "sparse" but not "query-dependent": relevance is measured against one report per slide, not against different questions. Query dependence is asserted by analogy to pathologist behaviour and is tested only indirectly via the TIS ablations (which are weak; see Sec. 4).
- Redundancy is partly a property of the encoder: CONCH is a vision-language-aligned encoder that maps morphologically similar tiles close together; the paper does not repeat Fig. 1 for other tile encoders (UNI, Virchow, DINOv2), so "WSIs are redundant" and "CONCH features are redundant" are conflated.

### Gaps
- Exact Fig. 1(a)–(c) values (nMSE at each K, neighbour-similarity fractions, histogram mode) are only in the figure images; the text gives just "K ≈ 250" and the 7.7/22.8/35.2% numbers.
- Which text encoder produced the report embedding q_rep in Fig. 1(d) is not stated explicitly in Supp. B (CONCH's text encoder is the natural guess but unconfirmed).

## 3. What is the central insight, and what method follows from it?

### Takeaway
Insight in two sentences: (1) a slide is a highly redundant bag of tile features, so the "slide-level encoder + long visual prefix" stack can be replaced by a small learned set of latents — but under weak slide-level text supervision a learned compressor collapses to a global average, so it must be taught to compress by self-supervised masked reconstruction with sparse attention and coverage/diversity pressure ("learning to compress"). (2) Because what matters is sparse and depends on the question, the LLM should not carry vision in its sequence at all: select a few query-relevant latents and inject them by gated cross-attention into a few early layers ("compress before fusion", "select-then-fuse").

### Cited Findings
- Design principles (verbatim): "(1) reduce token redundancy by compressing the long tile sequence into a compact latent set, and (2) select the most task-relevant latents for fusion with the pretrained LLM." — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Pipeline: frozen CONCH-v1 tile features (224×224 tiles at 20×, extracted once) → **STM** (Sparse Token Merger) → **MAE-pretrained resampler** → projector → **TIS** (Token Importance Scorer) picks Top-M of L latents → **CARA** (Cross-Attention Routing Adapter) injects them into LLM decoder layers {1,3,5,7} of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 3–4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- STM (local redundancy): non-overlapping s×s window sum-merge on the 2D tile grid with LayerNorm in/out, N' ≈ N/s^2 (default s=2); parameter-light, no learning of which tokens to merge. — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Resampler (global redundancy) framed as "scan and compress", mimicking expert slide reading: two LongNet (dilated-attention) context layers scan the full sequence; L learnable latent queries then aggregate via **Top-K sparse cross-attention** (K_top = 128 then 64; annealed to 64/32 during pretraining), "preventing the latents from collapsing to a global average over highly redundant inputs"; a latent self-attention layer decorrelates latents; 2D Fourier positional encoding on keys only; embedding dim 512. — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 3.1 + Supp. C.1, D.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Why MAE (authors' reasoning): end-to-end resampler+LLM training "usually requires millions of image-text pairs, which is rare for WSIs (around 30k WSIs in the TCGA dataset … and weak supervision)"; redundancy means "the resampler can reconstruct masked tiles from visible context without overfitting to noise"; "reconstruction on randomly masked indices prevents the resampler from outputting trivial averages and forces different latents to look at complementary regions." — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- MAE objective: 70% of tile tokens masked; a light 2-layer cross-attention decoder with position-indexed mask queries reconstructs the frozen tile *features* (not pixels) from the latents; loss = L_rec (MSE on masked tokens) + λ_cover·L_cover (hinge penalty on tiles whose max attention from any latent is below an adaptive threshold) + λ_feat·L_feat (squared off-diagonal latent covariance); λ_cover = 5e-4, λ_feat = 1e-3; 100k steps. — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 3.1, Supp. C.2–C.3, D.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- TIS: additive scorer ρ_i = w^T GELU(W_v v_i + W_q q̃) over projected latents and the mean-pooled text embedding; keeps Top-M. Trained with no extra labels by **self-distillation from CARA's own text→vision cross-attention** (averaged over heads, text positions and CARA layers; forward KL) plus a pairwise ranking loss; λ_TIS = 0.02, λ_rank = 0.5. — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 3.2 + Supp. C.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- CARA: H̃ = H + γ_ℓ·Attn(LN(H), V_I, V_I) with a learnable gate γ_ℓ initialised small; vision never enters the LLM token sequence. Complexity: LLaVA prefill O(n(T+L)^2) vs. O(nT^2 + n_× T M); KV cache O(nd(T+L+A)) vs. O(nd(T+A)) + O(n_× M d). — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 3.2 + Supp. C.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Token budgets: L = 256 or 128 latents, M = 96 routed latents (vs. 576 visual tokens for WSI-LLaVA, 20,480 for SlideChat, 2,000 for TCP-LLaVA, 64 for MLLM-HWSI). — [arXiv HTML v4, Tab. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Training recipe: (0) MAE pretraining on WSI-Bench slides (~9k WSIs); (1) alignment on WSI-Bench reports — resampler and LLM frozen, train projector+TIS+CARA, 3 epochs; (2) instruction tuning on all ~170k VQA pairs — unfreeze the resampler's last cross-attention layer, add LoRA (r=128, α=64) to the LLM, 2 epochs. Cost 85.84 + 4.49 + 117.20 = 207.53 GPU-hours on RTX 6000 Ada; every stage fits on a 24 GB GPU (peak 9.2 / 12.4 / 16.8 GB). — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 4.1 + Tab. S2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4); [GitHub README](https://github.com/WinstonHuTiger/loc_path)

### Inferences
- Why the idea "sounds right": (a) it matches the MIL tradition in pathology (attention highlights a few critical patches) and the pathologist workflow (scan at low power, then focus) — the paper leans on this analogy repeatedly ("scan and compress", "mimic human experts' query-dependent focus"); (b) redundancy makes masked reconstruction a well-posed pretext task for a compressor — if tiles are predictable from context, a small latent set can encode the slide; (c) with ~9k slides and one report each, the language loss alone is too weak a signal to shape a compressor, so a label-free objective is the natural fix; (d) moving vision out of the LLM sequence makes cost independent of slide size, which is the practical pain point.
- The real conceptual content is less "WSIs are redundant" (widely believed) and more **"the failure mode of learned compression under weak supervision is averaging; reconstruction + sparsity + coverage/diversity is what prevents it"**. The title's "learning to compress" refers to this, in contrast to heuristic/training-free merging or pruning.
- The method's two halves rest on different observations and have very different evidential support: the compression half (redundancy) is well supported; the routing half (query-dependent sparsity) is weakly supported (see Sec. 4).
- There is a tension between coverage regularisation (make latents cover the whole slide — a task-agnostic "summary") and the claim that relevance is sparse; the design resolves it by deferring selectivity to TIS, but TIS selects among latents that are each already spatial mixtures over up to 64–128 tiles, so selection granularity is coarse.

### Gaps
- No derivation or experiment showing how many latents are *needed* as a function of slide content (e.g., per-slide adaptive L or M); budgets are fixed globally. The authors list adaptive budgets as future work.

## 4. What evidence supports the insight itself?

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence is negative/ablative: (i) feeding 20,480 raw tile tokens to the same LLM is no better than 96 routed latents; (ii) compressors trained without MAE or without sparse attention collapse toward averages and lose a lot; (iii) even crude pooling loses only ~2–3 points. Together these say token count is not the quality lever on current slide-level benchmarks. Evidence for query-dependent routing (TIS) and for cross-attention being *more accurate* than concatenation is within noise; their benefit is efficiency.

### Cited Findings
- **Raw tokens vs. compressed (same Qwen2.5-7B)**: no-compression reference (up to 20,480 raw CONCH tokens) scores VQA Avg 0.732 / Clin. Avg 0.401; LoC-Path L=128/M=96 scores 0.740 / 0.429; L=256/M=96 scores 0.738 / 0.416. The raw-token baseline's training is estimated at ~357 GPU-h vs. 207.53 for all three LoC-Path stages (1.7×). Authors: "compressing to M = 96 routed latents does not hurt accuracy in this setting." — [arXiv HTML v4, Tab. 1, Sec. 4.2, Tab. S2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- **Parity with WSI-LLaVA, stated with uncertainty**: paired WSI-cluster bootstrap on 3,194 shared questions (442 WSIs): LoC-Path − reproduced WSI-LLaVA = −0.010 on VQA average (95% CI [−0.023, 0.003]) and −0.002 on VQA+clinical (CI [−0.020, 0.016]); "the two models are not statistically distinguishable on this subset." Published WSI-LLaVA VQA Avg 0.754, reproduced 0.746, LoC-Path 0.740; report-generation Clin. Avg 0.429 (LoC-Path) vs. 0.405/0.410 (WSI-LLaVA published/reproduced). — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 4.2, Tab. 1, Tab. S3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- **"Token count is not the main quality lever"**: pooling WSI-LLaVA's own 576 visual tokens down to 256 / 128 changes its VQA Avg only from 0.746 to 0.744 / 0.738. Authors: "This reinforces that the number of visual tokens is not the main quality lever in this regime." — [arXiv HTML v4, Supp. F.6, Tab. S9](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- **Learned vs. heuristic compression** (same projector/TIS/CARA and budget L=256, M=96; only the compressor swapped): VQA Avg — LoC-Path 0.738; ToMe 0.723; ACMIL 0.723; average pooling 0.713; DivPrune 0.650. - **MAE pretraining is necessary (the biggest effect in the paper)**: training the same resampler with LLM loss only (LLaVA-style) gives VQA Avg 0.668, Clin. Avg 0.199, VQA+clin 0.433 (vs. 0.738 / 0.416 / 0.577). — [arXiv HTML v4, Tab. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)

### Inferences
- My arithmetic check of the headline: mean TFLOPs of Quilt-LLaVA, SlideChat, WSI-LLaVA = (12.798 + 267.672 + 10.491)/3 ≈ 96.99, and 3.019/96.99 ≈ 3.1% → 96.9% lower; mean memory ≈ 12.61 GB, 7.709/12.61 ≈ 61% → 38.9% lower. So the "97%" is dominated by SlideChat's 20k-token prefix; against WSI-LLaVA alone the saving is ~71% TFLOPs / ~43% memory (and that comparison excludes WSI-LLaVA's slide encoder and LoC-Path's resampler).
- The most informative pattern for an "insight" reading: a 200× reduction in tokens (20,480 → 96) costs nothing, crude pooling costs ~2.5 points, yet a *badly trained* learned compressor costs 7–8 points on VQA and halves report quality. So how the compact representation is learned matters far more than how many tokens are kept — averaging is the enemy, not compression.
- The same pattern admits a deflationary reading: WSI-Bench-style questions (tumour type, grade, coarse morphology, guideline treatment) are largely answerable from a slide-level gist, so the benchmark may be insensitive to visual detail. The paper itself notes that token-reduction benchmark signal "is debated" for natural images, and the WSI-LLaVA pooling result (576 → 128 tokens, −0.008) is exactly what one would see on a gist-answerable benchmark.
- TIS's in-distribution effect (+0.002 / +0.003) is far below what single-seed runs can resolve; the only statistically supported TIS result is the OOD BCNB comparison against pooling the no-TIS model's latents, which is not the same as comparing against a model trained to use 96 pooled or randomly chosen latents.
- LLaVA-concat with the same latents (0.733) ≈ CARA (0.738): cross-attention routing buys efficiency and constant-memory scaling, not accuracy. The plain Flamingo variant is slightly *worse* than concat, so the specifics (early layers 1/3/5/7, gating, ranking loss) matter at the ~0.01 level.
- STM improving a linear lesion probe suggests neighbourhood averaging denoises per-tile CONCH features — local redundancy is not just removable but mildly harmful as noise for linear readout.

### Gaps
- No experiment isolates "query-dependence": e.g., routing with a shuffled/mismatched question, random Top-M, or measuring how much the selected latent set changes across different questions on the same slide.
- No seed variance; "All models are trained once with seed 42" and "all Tab. 1 entries are single runs" (authors' own caveat) — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 4.1–4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4). Ablation deltas of ≤0.01 cannot be interpreted.
- Memory/FLOPs for MLLM-HWSI and TCP-LLaVA were not profiled, so the efficiency comparison against the two other compression-based slide MLLMs is missing.

## 5. Limitations, unstated assumptions, weak points, open questions

### Takeaway
The paper (v4) is unusually candid: compression is lossy at the localisation level, quality is "on par" not better, fine-grained details (grades, biomarkers, Nottingham scores) are hallucinated or missed, and OOD accuracy lags simpler baselines. The deeper open question is whether "96 tokens suffice" is a fact about WSIs or about today's coarse, TCGA-report-derived benchmarks and LLM-judge metrics.

### Cited Findings
- Authors' stated limitations: "LoC-Path depends on a fixed tile encoder, so domain shift in the encoder propagates to the LLM. Its compression favors global coverage over worst-case preservation, so subtle lesions can be under-represented, and generated reports can be globally plausible while missing fine-grained details (e.g., grades or biomarker status) that LLM-judge metrics only partly penalize." Future work: objectives "that preserve rare but critical patterns", budgets adapted "to the question and slide", multi-institutional data "with pathologist evaluation and calibrated uncertainty". — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Lossy at localisation level: after the resampler, a linear lesion probe on back-projected latents collapses to FROC 0.065 on small-lesion CAMELYON16 slides (from 0.744 after STM; Δ −0.677, CI [−0.772, −0.574]); all-slides 0.268. The retained property is only that lesion tiles stay inside latent attention support (99.78% of lesion-positive groups attended by ≥1 latent; lesion groups get 2.77× proportional attention mass; 98.75% of lesion tiles covered after M=96 routing). Authors: "Compression is therefore lossy at the localization level … we claim retention only in this sense." — [arXiv HTML v4, Supp. F.9, Tab. S11](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Failure modes: "In treatment planning, the model tends to offer generic guideline-based regimens while under-emphasizing case-specific constraints like biomarkers … in report generation … it occasionally hallucinates fine-grained attributes (e.g., Nottingham scores or microcalcifications). Overall, LoC-Path reliably captures coarse diagnostic intent but lacks precision for granular decision-critical details." — [arXiv HTML v4, Supp. G.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- OOD weakness: zero-shot Slide-Bench (BCNB) accuracy — LoC-Path 0.519 vs. WSI-LLaVA 0.553, SlideChat 0.546, average pooling 0.545, DivPrune 0.544; the authors call this "a modest trade-off on Slide-Bench". Path-Bench subset: 0.847 (L=128) ties SlideChat 0.847. — [arXiv HTML v4, Tab. 1, Sec. 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Hyperparameter fragility: λ_TIS ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.05} moves WSI-Bench closed-task macro accuracy between 0.854 and 0.927 and diagnosis accuracy between 0.754 and 0.860; λ_rank is stable (0.915–0.927). — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 4.3, Tab. S10](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Evaluation depends on GPT-4o-as-judge metrics (WSI-Precision / WSI-Relevance from WSI-LLaVA); cross-judge rank agreement with Qwen-Plus and Claude Sonnet is reported as Spearman 0.92 (WSI-P) and 0.87 (WSI-R). — [arXiv HTML v4, Supp. E.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Data scale: resampler pretraining and all fusion training use only WSI-Bench (~9k TCGA WSIs, ~170k VQA pairs); the compared slide encoders "are pretrained on much larger private datasets … which likely explains the remaining gap." — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 4.1–4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- The Fig. 5 TIS interpretation is one post-hoc case using zero-shot CONCH tissue labels and keyword-matched report classes; the authors say it "should be interpreted as a weak report-alignment analysis rather than a ground-truth semantic attribution result." — [arXiv HTML v4, Supp. G.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Claim evolution across versions: v2 claimed TFLOPs "81.91% lower than all the general baselines" and described TIS's +0.002 as "a clear performance gain"; v4 re-measures SlideChat at 20,480 tokens (→ 96.9%), adds bootstrap CIs, the raw-token baseline, MLLM-HWSI/TCP-LLaVA rows and the CAMELYON16 test, and calls the TIS gain "small". — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v2); [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)

### Inferences
- Unstated assumption 1: tile-feature similarity ≈ informational redundancy. Two tiles with cosine 0.95 in CONCH space can still differ in clinically decisive ways (mitoses, a micrometastasis, grade-defining nuclear detail). The CAMELYON16 result (lesion not linearly recoverable from latents) is direct evidence that "redundant" features hide rare signal.
- Unstated assumption 2: MSE reconstruction of features is the right notion of "information to keep". MSE is dominated by common tissue; rare, small lesions contribute little to the loss. The coverage term guarantees attention *support*, not that the latent encodes the lesion — precisely the caveat the authors make.
- Unstated assumption 3: relevance can be decided from mean-pooled question text + a latent, by a one-layer scorer, before the LLM has reasoned at all, and once for the whole generation (no re-selection per decoding step or per layer).
- "Attention coverage ≥ 98%" for lesion tiles is a weak guarantee given overall tile coverage is already ~0.89 with K_top up to 64–128 tiles per latent and 128–256 latents: nearly everything is "covered".
- TIS is trained by distilling CARA's attention over the already-selected M latents, i.e., a self-reinforcing loop (latents never routed never receive a teacher signal). The paper does not discuss exploration or selection bias in this distillation.
- All training/evaluation is TCGA-derived except BCNB, and the method is worst there relative to baselines — consistent with a compressor fitted to TCGA feature statistics. The steady gain with larger M on BCNB suggests the M=96 budget is tuned to in-distribution data.
- The efficiency story is LLM-side prefill/KV-cache; in real deployments tile feature extraction over tens of thousands of tiles (excluded as "preprocessing") dominates wall-clock, so the practical win is mainly in *training* cost and GPU memory (24 GB-class GPUs), which is how v4 reframes the contribution ("resource-efficient … development and deployment").
- Open questions: Does the 96-token sufficiency survive benchmarks that need fine-grained or spatial evidence (grading, mitotic counts, margin status, micrometastasis, multi-slide cases)? Would query-conditioned compression *before* the resampler (rather than selection after a query-agnostic summary) preserve rare evidence? Can budgets be adaptive per slide/question? Does the redundancy profile hold for non-CONCH encoders and for non-TCGA (biopsy, frozen section, IHC) slides? Is multi-magnification information needed (the model uses only 20× tiles)?

### Gaps
- No pathologist evaluation, no multi-institution test, no calibration analysis (all listed by the authors as future work).
- No public peer-review text to confirm which weaknesses reviewers raised.

## 6. Positioning against prior slide-level MLLMs and general-domain token pruning

### Takeaway
LoC-Path positions itself as the "no slide encoder, no visual prefix" alternative: prior slide MLLMs either pay for a heavy slide-level encoder (PRISM/TITAN/GigaPath-style), a long LLaVA-style prefix (SlideChat, WSI-LLaVA), or a Q-Former bottleneck that still concatenates (ALPaCA, TCP-LLaVA), while general-domain token pruning (FastV etc.) is training-free and designed for natural images. Its claim is parity at a fraction of compute, not higher accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- Slide-level encoders (TITAN, PRISM, GigaPath, COBRA, etc.): "every slide-level encoder is an additional heavy model stacked on the tile encoder; we instead absorb its role into a lightweight resampler while maintaining its function." They are compared only as frozen feature extractors on BRCA MIL tasks (Tab. 2), not as MLLMs. — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 2, Tab. 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Patch-level assistants: PathChat, Quilt-LLaVA, PA-LLaVA and CPath-Omni are grouped as models that "answer questions about individual tiles"; Quilt-LLaVA is run on 1024×1024 WSI thumbnails as a baseline (VQA Avg 0.721, Slide-Bench 0.163, Path-Bench 0.380). PathChat and CPath-Omni are cited but not benchmarked. — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 2, Sec. 4.1, Tab. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- SlideChat and WSI-LLaVA: characterised as LLaVA-style late fusion ("concatenate-and-attend"), which "becomes increasingly inefficient and difficult to train under weak slide-level supervision"; slide-level systems "often inherit the two-encoder pipeline and quadratic prefilling costs". Numbers: SlideChat VQA Avg 0.495 (zero-shot on WSI-Bench, 20,480 tokens, 267.7 TFLOPs), WSI-LLaVA 0.754 published / 0.746 reproduced (576 tokens, 10.5 TFLOPs), LoC-Path 0.740 (96 routed tokens, 3.0 TFLOPs). — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 1–2, Tab. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Q-Former-bottleneck slide MLLMs (ALPaCA, TCP-LLaVA): reduce tokens entering the LLM "with a learned bottleneck" but stay in the same regime; TCP-LLaVA (2,000 query tokens, authors' reproduction) VQA Avg 0.499; MLLM-HWSI (64 tokens, official checkpoint) 0.606. — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 1, Tab. 1, Supp. E.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Agentic alternative: CPathAgent "sidesteps the slide-level encoder by driving a single vision encoder through iterative navigation, yet its multi-turn loop can add inference latency"; LoC-Path claims a single forward pass. — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- General-domain token reduction: three lines — merging (ToMe, ToFu, PiToMe), pruning/selection (FastV low-attention pruning, PyramidDrop, VTW, SparseVLM, FitPrune, DivPrune, PACT, Dynamic-LLaVA, LLaVA-PruMerge, AIM, DyCoke, LLaVA-Meteor), and query-based resampling (Flamingo Perceiver Resampler, BLIP-2 Q-Former, TokenPacker, Victor). Critique: "Most operate as training-free, post-hoc steps on natural images, on benchmarks whose token-reduction signal is debated"; FOCUS compresses tile tokens in pathology but for few-shot WSI classification, not MLLM generation. LoC-Path's contrast: "two trainable mechanisms tailored to gigapixel WSIs: a query-based resampler … at the vision side, and a Top-M router that selects tokens inside the LLM via a cross-attention adapter, jointly yielding linear-complexity vision–language fusion." — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- Empirical comparison to token-reduction methods uses ToMe (AIM implementation), DivPrune, ACMIL attention selection and adaptive average pooling as drop-in replacements for STM+resampler (results in Sec. 4 above). FastV-style in-LLM attention pruning is cited but **not** run as a baseline. — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 4.1, Supp. E.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)
- GPT-4o on thumbnails as a generalist reference: VQA Avg 0.507, Clin. Avg 0.103. — [arXiv HTML v4, Tab. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05391v4)

### Inferences
- The positioning is architectural/economic rather than accuracy-driven: "you do not need TITAN/PRISM-scale slide encoders or 20k-token prefixes to match WSI-LLaVA on WSI-Bench; ~208 GPU-hours on 24 GB cards suffice." This is the claim to carry forward, with the caveat that parity is shown on one in-distribution benchmark.
- Conceptual difference from FastV-type work: FastV observes that LLM attention to visual tokens decays in deeper layers and prunes *inside* the LLM, training-free. LoC-Path never lets visual tokens into the sequence, decides relevance *before* the LLM with a learned scorer, and restricts vision to early layers 1/3/5/7. Its finding that early-to-mid CARA placement beats layers 1/8/15/22 is loosely consistent with the FastV intuition that visual information is consumed early, but the paper does not make this link or analyse LLM attention.
- Classifying CPath-Omni as patch-level is a simplification (its own title describes unified patch and WSI analysis); PRISM/TITAN are treated purely as encoders, although both have language-aligned/generative components. The comparison set for slide-level MLLMs is thus narrower than the related-work list suggests.
- SlideChat's poor WSI-Bench numbers (0.495) reflect zero-shot evaluation of a model trained on different data, whereas LoC-Path and WSI-LLaVA are trained on WSI-Bench; only the WSI-LLaVA and raw-token comparisons are like-for-like.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head with PathChat, CPath-Omni, PRISM or TITAN as report generators/VQA systems, and no FastV/PyramidDrop/SparseVLM baselines applied to the raw-token Qwen model — so "learned compression beats in-LLM pruning for WSIs" is not tested.
- No comparison with agentic/navigation approaches (CPathAgent) on accuracy or latency; the latency argument against them is asserted, not measured.
