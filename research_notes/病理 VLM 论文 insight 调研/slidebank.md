# SlideBank (pathology WSI VLM paper) — identification and insight notes

Scope note: the full paper was read end to end (arXiv HTML v1 including appendices A–D, plus PDF page 1 for affiliations). Sections "Inferences" contain my own analysis and arithmetic, not statements by the authors. No OpenReview page, peer reviews, GitHub repo or project page were found, so there is no external critique to report.

## 1. Exactly which paper is "SlideBank"?

### Takeaway
There is an exact title match and it is unambiguous: "SlideBank: A Persistent Hierarchical Evidence Bank for Consistent Whole-Slide Reasoning" (arXiv:2609.00342, 31 Aug 2026, Xiaoxiao Li's group at UBC / Vector Institute). It is a training-free, agent-style framework wrapped around an existing pathology VLM, not a newly trained VLM; no venue, code or reviews are public yet.

### Cited Findings

No conference/journal is named in the comments field. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00342)

### Inferences
- Confidence that arXiv:2609.00342 is the intended paper is high: exact name, pathology WSI + VLM topic, inside the 2024–2026 window, and the only arXiv hit. If the researcher actually meant a benchmark, the alternative is SlideBench (SlideChat).
- "VLM paper" is a slightly loose label: SlideBank trains nothing and contributes a slide-level evidence representation plus retrieval/fusion procedure around frozen Quilt-LLaVA or Patho-R1.

### Gaps
- Venue/acceptance status unknown (preprint only as of 2026-11-20); no OpenReview entry located.
- No public code, so prompts and ontology in the appendix are the only implementation detail available; the lexical routing rules and the description-to-signal mapping procedure are not released.

## 2. Problem framing and motivating observation (the intro "story")

### Takeaway
The story is a deliberate shift of attention from "how do we acquire evidence from a gigapixel slide" to "what form should the acquired evidence take once exploration is over". The authors claim that both dominant paradigms throw away the product of exploration: slide encoders keep it as opaque latents, agents keep it as trajectory-specific transient state, so nothing persists that a second, differently worded question could look up and trace back to pixels.

### Cited Findings
- Premise about WSIs: "Diagnostically relevant findings are often sparse, spatially dispersed, and highly heterogeneous: a small focus of atypical cells, an invasive tissue interface, or a mitotic hotspot may occupy only a tiny fraction of the entire slide while being decisive for diagnosis. Consequently, directly compressing a WSI into a thumbnail or uniformly processing a limited set of patches can easily discard critical evidence." — [arXiv HTML §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- What reasoning requires: "not only understanding what is visible, but also determining where diagnostically informative evidence lies, at what scale it should be examined, and how evidence distributed across the slide should be organized for downstream reasoning." — [arXiv HTML §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Diagnosis of prior work (two families): WSI-level VLMs "aggregate precomputed patch or slide features into compact latent representations"; pathology agents treat WSI understanding "as an active evidence-acquisition problem, using planning, region selection, and multi-scale navigation". — [arXiv HTML §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- The claimed gap: "the information retained after slide exploration is typically represented either as latent visual features, flat collections of observations, or states optimized for a particular navigation or reasoning trajectory. How to organize visually established evidence from the current slide into an explicit representation that can be semantically accessed by different downstream questions remains comparatively underexplored." — [arXiv HTML §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Central question, verbatim: "how can evidence acquired from a gigapixel WSI be transformed into a persistent representation that supports diverse downstream questions while preserving explicit traceability to the underlying visual observations?" followed by "This perspective shifts the focus from evidence acquisition alone to how acquired evidence is organized and accessed after exploration." — [arXiv HTML §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Four stated challenges: (1) exhaustive inspection is infeasible but aggressive sampling misses sparse abnormalities, so one needs "a compact but diagnostically diverse set of fields across both architectural and cellular scales"; (2) vocabulary mismatch — "the language used in downstream questions does not necessarily match the language of raw visual observations. Questions about nuclear grade, pleomorphism, or cellular atypia, for example, may rely on overlapping morphological signals even when their surface forms differ, making direct retrieval over free-form descriptions unreliable"; (3) evidence is multi-scale and spatial, so a finding "should remain linked not only to a textual description, but also to the anchor region, magnification, visual field, and WSI coordinates"; (4) different questions need different mixes of global/regional/cellular evidence, so the interface must be "persistent while supporting query-adaptive composition". — [arXiv HTML §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Abstract version of the gap: existing models/agents "can aggregate slide features or actively acquire evidence, but the information retained after exploration is often difficult to access semantically while preserving its connection to the original visual evidence." — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00342)

### Inferences
- The framing is a "representation of the slide" argument, not a "better model" argument: the object of study is the intermediate artefact left behind after looking at a slide. This is close to how a pathologist's worksheet/synoptic notes function — look once, record findings with locations, answer many questions from the record.
- The intro does not present an empirical motivating observation (no pilot experiment showing e.g. that agents give inconsistent answers or that free-text retrieval fails). The motivation is argued conceptually; the inconsistency evidence only appears later in Table 3, and only for non-agent baselines.

### Gaps
- No quantitative motivating study in the introduction; whether free-form description retrieval is in fact "unreliable" is asserted, never measured.

## 3. Central insight and the method that follows from it

### Takeaway
Insight in one sentence: separate slide exploration from question answering, and make the product of exploration an explicit, slide-specific, pixel-linked database indexed by a small controlled vocabulary of morphological "signals", so that any question can be mapped (question → clinical concept → signals → anchors/patches/coordinates) to the same stored evidence. Everything else — coarse-to-fine sampling, three-branch voting, bounded dialogue history — is plumbing in service of that "explore once, index by pathology concepts, reuse for every question" idea.

### Cited Findings
- Design principle: "SlideBank therefore separates slide exploration from question answering." Three components: agent-driven hierarchical bank construction; hierarchical evidence-augmented reasoning; persistent reuse across queries. — [arXiv HTML §3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Bank definition: M_s = (G_s, A_s, P_s, S_s, Ω) — global record, retained anchor regions, multi-scale patch views, grounded signal records, and a concept-to-signal ontology. — [arXiv HTML §3.2, Eq. 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Construction, step by step (question-independent):
  - Global survey: lowest pyramid level as thumbnail; HSV tissue masking; non-overlapping 384×384 thumbnail tiles (≥20% tissue kept); each tile assigned to one of five coarse classes (tumor, necrosis, adipose, stroma, other) "using handcrafted morphological statistics and foreground occupancy"; tumor Feret diameter/principal axis estimated when pixel spacing is known; all stored in G_s.
  - Coarse-to-fine "agentic" sampling: tiles ranked by diagnostic priority/triage confidence/coverage under a category-aware budget; then recursive 4×4 grid refinement in which the VLM scores 16 cells to pick anchors (3 per tumor-like tile, 2 per other tile), and again within each anchor to pick a ~20× fine-detail view (512 px at level 0) and an independently chosen ~10× architectural view; every view is resized to 256×256 for the VLM.
  - Observation: the pathology VLM writes a 2–3 sentence morphology-only caption per view with self-reported confidence and QC flag; low-confidence/invalid ones are dropped. The prompt forbids diagnosis, grade, subtype, biomarker statements "so that the bank stores observations", and states the organ "to suppress the organ drift that pathology VLMs exhibit on isolated fields".
  - Signal grounding: anchor descriptions are mapped onto a fixed vocabulary of localized signals; each record σ_{a,k} = (status ∈ {present, absent, unknown}, confidence, supporting patch views). — [arXiv HTML §3.2, §4 Implementation Details, App. A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- The ontology (breast-specific): 14 question-side concepts (M01–M11 morphology, MD01–MD02 metadata/size, P01 biomarker/outcome proxy) mapped many-to-many onto 14 "atomic morphologic" signals, e.g. Tumor grade → {S101 high nuclear pleomorphism, S102 high mitotic activity, S103 low tubule formation, S104 support for high tumor grade}; Margin status → {S141, S142}; three concepts (TILs/inflammation, perineural invasion, benign background) have "No dedicated signal; multi-scale evidence". The appendix notes the tag list "can be replaced by the ontology of the target cohort for other organs". — [arXiv HTML App. B, Tables 4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Inference: a "deterministic router ψ … based on lexical rules over the pathology concept vocabulary" returns the top-n concepts (n=3); their signals select anchors where the signal is "present" (preferred) and highest-confidence; evidence E(q) = (global record, anchors, patches, signals). — [arXiv HTML §3.3, Eq. 5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Answering: each evidence item (thumbnail; anchor image + stored description; patch image + stored caption) is scored independently by the same VLM on the multiple-choice options; per-item confidence = max option probability from logits; top-5 items per local branch; majority vote within anchor and patch branches; final score F(o) = 0.2·global + 0.4·anchor + 0.4·patch, fixed across datasets/backbones. Tumor-size questions are special-cased and answered from the thumbnail with a scale prior. — [arXiv HTML §3.3 Eq. 7–12, App. A.2, App. D](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Persistence: the bank is built once; "Routing depends only on the current question, so the retrieved evidence for a given question is identical whether it is asked in isolation or within a conversation"; only the previous Q–A pair is carried as dialogue context. — [arXiv HTML §3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- No training anywhere; the same frozen backbone (Quilt-LLaVA or Patho-R1) is used for construction and inference; single A100 80 GB. — [arXiv HTML §4 Implementation Details](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)

### Inferences
- Why the idea "sounds right": (a) it mirrors clinical practice — a slide is examined once, findings are recorded in standard terminology with locations, and many downstream questions (grade, margin, LVI) are answered from those findings; (b) it imports a well-understood systems idea — index once offline, query many times online — into WSI reasoning, which yields determinism and amortised cost almost by construction; (c) a controlled vocabulary acts as a concept-bottleneck between free-form questions and free-form captions, which is the classic remedy for vocabulary mismatch in retrieval; (d) keeping pixel coordinates in the record makes the answer auditable, which matters more in pathology than raw accuracy.
- The "signal layer" is the actual novelty relative to an agent that caches captions: it is a typed key (signal id + status + confidence) rather than text, so retrieval is a table lookup rather than similarity search. The paper explicitly links this to concept bottleneck models in related work.
- Note the conceptual tension: question-independent exploration is what buys persistence, but it also means evidence that the fixed prompt cues and fixed ontology did not anticipate will never be in the bank.

### Gaps
- The paper does not specify how descriptions are converted to signal status/confidence (rules, the VLM's tag outputs from prompt P2, or an LLM call); the lexical routing rules are said to be "in Appendix" but the appendix lists only the vocabulary.

## 4. Evidence that the insight (not just the system) is right

### Takeaway
The support is modest and mostly indirect: SlideBank lifts its own frozen backbones well above thumbnail-only use and roughly ties the best trained/agentic baselines, signal-guided access beats random access from the same bank by 0.7–2.6 points, and rephrasing consistency is ~99%. The consistency and amortised-cost results are the most distinctive evidence for the "persistent bank" thesis; the accuracy evidence for the "signal index" thesis is thin.

### Cited Findings
- Benchmarks: WSI-VQA (85 WSIs, 388 four-option questions after multiple-choice conversion) and SlideBench-BCNB (1,058 breast WSIs; 3 tasks used: tumor type 1,058 Q with 3 options, histological grading 926 Q with 3 options, molecular subtype 1,058 Q with 4 options; 3,042 Q total; question-count-weighted average). — [arXiv HTML §4, App. C.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Main numbers (Table 1): SlideBank w/ Patho-R1: WSI-VQA 52.77, BCNB avg 50.36 (tumor 83.27 / grade 40.06 / subtype 26.47). SlideBank w/ Quilt-LLaVA: WSI-VQA 43.75, BCNB avg 50.92 (89.82 / 33.56 / 27.22). Thumbnail-only backbones: Patho-R1 WSI-VQA 44.28, BCNB 39.95 (61.11 / 29.93 / 27.57); Quilt-LLaVA WSI-VQA 27.65, BCNB 33.93 (44.33 / 32.18 / 25.05). Strongest baselines: SlideChat WSI-VQA 52.05, BCNB 43.14 (87.99 / 17.86 / 20.41); PathAgent WSI-VQA 52.05, BCNB 41.08 (50.76 / 46.00 / 27.08); MedAgents (GPT-4o, thumbnail) BCNB 47.72 (89.38 / 37.37 / 15.12), WSI-VQA 42.75; TITAN BCNB 44.67, WSI-VQA 43.12; WSI-LLaVA WSI-VQA 49.61. — [arXiv HTML Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Authors' reading: results "show that organizing gigapixel WSI observations into a reusable structured evidence bank preserves and can improve standard single-query reasoning performance" (i.e. the claim is that a question-independent bank does not lose needed information). — [arXiv HTML §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Hierarchy ablation (Patho-R1, Table 2): global only 48.55 / 40.57 (WSI-VQA / BCNB); anchor only 51.98 / 50.23; patch only 51.45 / 48.06; full G+A+P 52.77 / 50.36. — [arXiv HTML Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Access ablation (the key test of the insight): same bank, signal-guided retrieval replaced by "uniformly sampling 32 patches at random": random 50.13 / 49.62 vs signal-guided 52.77 / 50.36. — [arXiv HTML §4.2, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Concept-count sensitivity (Fig. 2, Patho-R1, WSI-VQA): 49.9% with one concept, peak 52.8% at three, gradual decline beyond; "remains above random sampling across a broad range of 2–6 concepts"; interpreted as a coverage vs retrieval-noise trade-off. — [arXiv HTML §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Multi-turn / rephrasing (Table 3; 382 source questions × 6 forms = 2,292 turns from 80 WSIs, ~29 turns per slide, rephrasings by Gemini-2.5-Flash with shuffled options): SlideBank w/ Quilt-LLaVA acc 42.37 (Δ −1.38), consistency 99.47; w/ Patho-R1 acc 48.94 (Δ −3.83), consistency 99.21. Baselines: SlideChat 42.50 (Δ −9.55), cons. 88.69; WSI-LLaVA 17.06 (Δ −32.55), 73.21; Patho-R1 41.93 (Δ −2.35), 80.94; Quilt-LLaVA 25.22 (Δ −2.43), 84.25; PathGen-LLaVA 23.06 (Δ −16.84), 87.27; LLaVA-Med 16.18 (Δ −15.69), 62.83. Consistency for a question = 1 if the most frequent answer across the six forms occurs at least 3 times. — [arXiv HTML §4 Metrics, §4.4, Table 3, App. D](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Efficiency (Fig. 4, Quilt-LLaVA): average runtime falls "from 94.3 s for a single query to 5.9 s per query over the question sequence, approaching an online reasoning cost of 2.73 s per query". TITAN and agentic methods are excluded from this comparison. — [arXiv HTML §4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Traceability is shown only qualitatively: Fig. 3 gives one grading question traced to grade-related signals, regions and multi-magnification patches. — [arXiv HTML §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)

### Inferences
- What the evidence does establish: (a) feeding a frozen patch-level pathology VLM curated high-resolution fields beats feeding it a thumbnail (+8.5 pts Patho-R1 and +16.1 pts Quilt-LLaVA on WSI-VQA) — expected, and not specific to the bank idea; (b) fixed stored evidence plus deterministic routing gives near-perfect answer stability under rephrasing; (c) per-question cost becomes small once the bank exists (my check: (94.3 − 2.73)/29 + 2.73 ≈ 5.9 s, consistent with the reported figure).
- The direct test of the "signal index" insight is the random-vs-signal ablation, and its effect size is small: +2.64 pts on WSI-VQA is about 10 of 388 questions, +0.74 pts on BCNB is about 22 of 3,042; no confidence intervals, seeds or significance tests are reported. With a single concept, routing (49.9) is below random sampling (50.13), so the signal index helps only when it is broadened to three concepts.
- The hierarchy claim is also weakly supported: on BCNB the full hierarchy (50.36) is +0.13 over anchor-only (50.23); on WSI-VQA +0.79 (≈3 questions).
- The headline "best on WSI-VQA" is a margin of 0.72 pts over SlideChat and PathAgent (both 52.05), i.e. ~3 questions out of 388.
- BCNB gains are concentrated in tumor typing. Grading (33.56 Quilt, 40.06 Patho-R1; 3 options → 33.3% chance) and molecular subtype (27.22, 26.47; 4 options → 25% chance) are at or near chance, and subtype with Patho-R1 is lower with the bank (26.47) than with the thumbnail alone (27.57). The task where the ontology is richest (tumor grade → S101–S104) is exactly where the gain is smallest for Quilt-LLaVA (+1.4 pts), which is evidence against the signal layer capturing grade-relevant morphology with that backbone.
- Global-only SlideBank (48.55 on WSI-VQA) already exceeds thumbnail-only Patho-R1 (44.28) although both see only a thumbnail; about half of the +8.49 gain therefore seems to come from something other than local evidence (candidates: logit-based option scoring vs generative answer parsing, the special size-question branch, a different thumbnail). The paper does not explain this gap.
- The consistency metric is lenient. For a uniformly random 4-option answerer, the probability that some option appears ≥3 times in 6 draws is 1 − 1440/4096 ≈ 64.8% (my computation), so LLaVA-Med's 62.83 is chance level and the informative range of the metric is roughly 65–100%. SlideBank's ~99% is still clearly the best, but it is partly guaranteed by design (deterministic lexical router, fixed sampling seed 42, majority voting over several items) and says nothing about correctness: with Patho-R1 the system is ~99% consistent while ~51% of answers are wrong.
- On accuracy drop under multi-turn, the bank is not uniformly better than its own backbone: SlideBank w/ Patho-R1 loses 3.83 pts while thumbnail Patho-R1 loses 2.35.

### Gaps
- No ablation isolates the pieces the insight depends on: signal index vs embedding/free-text retrieval over the same captions; agentic sampling vs random or attention-based tile selection for bank construction; image+description vs image-only inputs; fusion-weight sensitivity; bank-size/budget sensitivity.
- No quantitative validation of signal correctness or spatial grounding (no pathologist audit, no localisation metric) even though traceability is the central promise.
- Bank statistics (number of anchors/patches/signals per slide, storage size, construction-time breakdown) are not reported beyond the 94.3 s single-query figure.

## 5. Limitations, unstated assumptions, weak points, open questions

### Takeaway
The argument's weakest links are (i) the evaluation is breast-only, multiple-choice, and small, with margins of a few questions; (ii) the "ontology" that carries the insight is a hand-written, 14-signal breast vocabulary with a lexical router, so generality is assumed rather than shown; and (iii) question-independent exploration plus a closed vocabulary bakes in what can ever be answered. The paper has no limitations section.

### Cited Findings
- Both benchmarks are breast pathology and the ontology is explicitly breast-specific: the concept vocabulary "covers common morphology-centered questions in breast pathology"; prompts list breast cues (DCIS, comedo necrosis, tubule formation, microcalcification); other organs require swapping in "the ontology of the target cohort". — [arXiv HTML App. A.1, App. B.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Routing is not learned: "ψ is based on lexical rules over the pathology concept vocabulary". — [arXiv HTML §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Signals are described as "affirmative, locally assessable morphologic findings rather than diagnostic labels", yet the vocabulary includes S104 "Support for high tumor grade — Combined visual evidence consistent with a higher-grade appearance", S151 "Lymph-node metastasis present" and S152 "Micrometastasis present"; S151/S152 are not mapped to any concept in Table 4. — [arXiv HTML App. B.2, Tables 4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Non-morphological targets are routed to morphology proxies: concept P01 "Biomarker or outcome proxy prediction" → S101–S104, S111, S113, S132; MD02 tumor size → "S141 (weak proxy), with global evidence"; three concepts have no dedicated signal at all. — [arXiv HTML Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Resolution choices: every view (384-px thumbnail tile, up to 4096-px level-0 context crop, 512-px "~20×" view, "~10×" view) is "resized to 256×256 for VLM processing"; tile classes come from handcrafted statistics. — [arXiv HTML §3.2, §4 Implementation Details](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Filtering and ranking rely on the VLM's own confidence: a "self-reported confidence score" for descriptions and token-probability confidence for answer candidates. — [arXiv HTML §3.2, §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Size questions use a hard-coded prior in the prompt ("Original WSI long side is about 50000 px. Thumbnail long side is about 1000 px (about 50x compression)"). — [arXiv HTML App. A.2, prompt P6](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Evaluation is closed-set multiple choice only (answers parsed to A–D; unparseable counted wrong); no report generation or open-ended QA. Baseline VLMs receive only a 1024×1024 thumbnail "without access to additional high-resolution fields". — [arXiv HTML §4 Baselines, App. C.2, App. D](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- The nearest methodological relatives named in related work (PathNavigate, GIANT, SlideSeek, HistoSelect, BEACON, AdaptivePath, WSI-Agents, PathFinder) are not in the comparison tables; the only WSI-exploring agent baseline is PathAgent, and agentic methods are excluded from the runtime/consistency comparison. — [arXiv HTML §2.1, Table 1, §4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- SlideChat's own paper reports 54.15% on SlideBench-VQA (BCNB) over its full BCNB question set (7 task types), whereas SlideBank reports SlideChat at 43.14% on a 3-task subset, so the two BCNB numbers are not comparable. — [SlideChat arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11761); [arXiv HTML Table 1, App. C.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)

### Inferences
- Unstated assumptions: (1) a fixed, organ-specific signal vocabulary can anticipate the questions that will be asked — the benefit of persistence shrinks if questions fall outside it (the "no dedicated signal" rows already fall back to generic evidence); (2) a frozen patch-level VLM produces reliable, calibrated morphological captions and tags on 256×256 inputs — the bank is only as good as these, and an early mis-description becomes persistent, consistently reproduced error (consistency then amplifies rather than corrects it); (3) question-independent sampling finds what matters — rare foci missed at construction time can never be recovered at query time because there is no re-exploration fallback; (4) a 512-px level-0 crop shrunk to 256×256 (effectively about 10× on a 40× scan) is enough to judge "high mitotic activity" or nuclear pleomorphism, which is doubtful and consistent with near-chance grading for Quilt-LLaVA.
- The paper's own framing ("vocabulary mismatch makes free-text retrieval unreliable") is undercut by using lexical rules on the question side: paraphrase robustness of routing is exactly what a lexical router is bad at, and the 99% consistency was measured on LLM paraphrases that likely keep the key pathology terms.
- "Agent-driven" is generous: there is no planning, tool use or iterative decision loop; the pipeline is a fixed three-prompt cascade over 4×4 grids with heuristic tile triage.
- "Training-free" also means the method inherits backbone ceilings: the best configuration differs by benchmark (Quilt-LLaVA best on BCNB, Patho-R1 best on WSI-VQA), and results are far from clinically useful (≈50%).
- Thumbnail-only baselines are a weak comparison class for a method that reads high-magnification crops; the fair comparisons are the slide-level and agentic rows, where SlideBank ties (WSI-VQA) or wins mainly via one task (BCNB tumor type, where a GPT-4o thumbnail agent also reaches 89.38). A class-imbalance explanation for the ~88–90% cluster on tumor type is plausible but I did not verify BCNB class frequencies.
- Open questions the paper leaves: Can the signal ontology be induced or extended automatically and across organs? Should the bank be updatable (query-triggered re-exploration when retrieved evidence is "unknown"/low confidence)? Does a typed signal index beat dense retrieval over stored captions? How accurate are the stored signals and coordinates against pathologist annotation? Does the persistent-bank idea still help once the backbone is a strong slide-level model, or for open-ended report generation and multi-slide cases? How does it compare with PathNavigate's question-independent scan + latent slide memory, the closest competitor?

### Gaps
- No peer reviews or author discussion of limitations exist to corroborate these critiques; all weak points above are my reading of the paper.
- BCNB tumor-type class distribution not checked, so the "majority-class" explanation is unverified.

## 6. Positioning relative to prior pathology VLM work

### Takeaway
SlideBank positions itself orthogonally to model-building papers: PathChat/Quilt-LLaVA/CONCH are "localized field" models that cannot ingest a WSI, SlideChat/WSI-LLaVA/TITAN/PRISM2/MLLM-HWSI compress the slide into model-internal features, and agents (PathFinder, SlideSeek, GIANT, PathAgent, PathNavigate) hold evidence only as transient reasoning state. SlideBank's claimed distinct niche is an externally materialised, inspectable, reusable per-slide evidence store sitting on top of any patch-level VLM.

### Cited Findings
- Patch-level generation: "CONCH learns transferable visual–language representations, while Quilt-LLaVA and PathChat support instruction following and open-ended interaction with histology images. Because these models cannot directly process a complete WSI at native resolution, subsequent methods aggregate pre-extracted patch features into slide-level representations." — [arXiv HTML §2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Slide-level generation: "WSI-VQA introduced generative question answering over WSIs, TITAN learned a multimodal whole-slide representation, and SlideChat and WSI-LLaVA connected slide features to language models"; "MLLM-HWSI aligns cell-, patch-, region-, and slide-level tokens with pathology language, while ALPaCA and PRISM2 scale slide-level supervision through question answering and clinical dialogue". — [arXiv HTML §2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Agents: "PathFinder, WSI-Agents, SlideSeek, GIANT, and PathAgent introduce specialized agents, planning, tool use, iterative navigation, or evidence synthesis"; HistoSelect (question-guided coarse-to-fine selection), BEACON (expected information gain), AdaptivePath (question-agnostic multiscale navigation); "EviPathBench further shows that locating diagnostic regions remains substantially harder than reasoning over preselected evidence". — [arXiv HTML §2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Closest prior work and the stated difference: "PathNavigate is particularly related to our setting because it performs a question-independent scan and maintains an online memory over frozen slide features before question-conditioned search. However, existing methods primarily represent acquired evidence as model-internal features or transient reasoning states. SlideBank instead materializes selected multiscale image fields, morphological descriptions, pathology signals, and WSI coordinates as an independently inspectable bank that can be retrieved across multiple questions about the same slide." — [arXiv HTML §2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Versus hierarchy and memory work: HIPT and H2-MIL "primarily structure latent visual features"; concept bottleneck models are cited as the interpretable-intermediate precedent; SurvAgent (cross-case bank) and PathMem (domain knowledge as long-term memory) "focus on latent hierarchies, general episodic memory, cross-case experience, or domain knowledge rather than persistent spatial evidence from repeated examination of one WSI". — [arXiv HTML §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Versus dialogue history: "every turn is answered from the persistent evidence bank, not only affected by the accumulated conversation history." — [arXiv HTML §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Used as components or baselines rather than competitors: Quilt-LLaVA and Patho-R1 are the frozen backbones; SlideChat, WSI-LLaVA, TITAN are "WSI-trained" baselines; SlideChat's SlideBench-BCNB is one of the two benchmarks. — [arXiv HTML §4, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)
- Not cited at all in the paper: CPath-Omni, the original PRISM (only PRISM2 / Vorontsov et al. 2026 appears), PathAlign, Prov-GigaPath, HistGen/report-generation lines. PathChat is cited only as a patch-level assistant (Lu et al., Nature 2024) and is not evaluated. — [arXiv HTML References](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00342)

### Inferences
- In a taxonomy of pathology-VLM insights, SlideBank belongs to the "inference-time organisation / agentic memory" branch, not the "data scale" (Quilt, PathGen), "slide encoder + LLM" (SlideChat, WSI-LLaVA, PRISM, TITAN) or "unified patch+slide model" (CPath-Omni) branches. Its implicit bet is that the bottleneck in WSI QA is not the VLM's perception but the interface between a gigapixel slide and a patch-level VLM — and that this interface should be symbolic and auditable rather than learned and latent.
- It is complementary to slide-level models in principle (a bank could be built with any backbone), but the paper never tests a slide-level or stronger model as the reader, so the complementarity is untested.
- Relative to PathNavigate, the delta is representation form (explicit records + ontology vs latent slide memory), not the scan-once idea itself; without a head-to-head comparison the paper cannot show that explicit signals are better than latent memory, only that they are more inspectable.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head numbers against PathNavigate, GIANT, SlideSeek, HistoSelect, PRISM2, MLLM-HWSI, ALPaCA or CPath-Omni; positioning against them is purely narrative.
- I did not read the cited 2026 agent papers (PathNavigate, BEACON, AdaptivePath, EviPathBench), so their characterisation here is SlideBank's own description, not independently verified.
